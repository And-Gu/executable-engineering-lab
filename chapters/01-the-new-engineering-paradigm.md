## The one question
How do executable models, software-driven workflows and AI agents change the systems we build **and the way we engineer them**?

## What the reader must gain
- Recognize systems engineering as the whole product across disciplines, people, interfaces and real use.
- Understand two co-evolving systems: the **product** and the **engineering environment** (people, models, methods, tools, checks, agents and delivery workflows).
- Explain how a useful model/test/analysis becomes reusable capability; why faster feedback is not simply more code.
- Distinguish AI agents used in engineering from **optional AI in a product**; an intersection need not be AI-controlled.
- Understand why software/model revisions and physical hardware/prototype verification have different cadences.

## Opening engineering situation
A city wants to improve traffic through a fictional four-way intersection without assuming a known correct controller. Traffic patterns, conflicting movements, road users, installation, verification needs and potential signal strategies are still uncertain. Begin with the question, not a specification, AI classifier or coding exercise.

## Narrative and explanation
1. One engineering intent creates questions about the traffic-control product **and** about what the team can model, observe, test and change.
2. Show three *conceptual* findings: uneven arrivals, an unmodeled movement/road-user conflict, and a proposed software timing change with physical/interface implications.
3. For each finding, explain one consequence for the product and one for engineering capabilities. Use the new dark site's product/engineering-environment diagram pattern without forcing a checklist.
4. Introduce systems engineering, executable engineering, software-driven workflows, AI assistance and agentic work **in context**, not a glossary dump.
5. Compare engineering AI (agent prepares/inspects candidate experiments) with a hypothetical **product AI** option (prediction for adaptive signaling). Neither is part of the initial simplified reference model; not all software automation is agentic.
6. Show different development and validation cadences and how learning changes next decisions.

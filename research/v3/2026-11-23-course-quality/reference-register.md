# Useful change, feedback and engineering investment

<!-- EEL_RESEARCH_NOT_FOR_PUBLICATION -->

Research ID EEL-R-2026-11-23-Q86. Publication status `background-only`. Primary material checked November 23, 2026. This register accompanies #86 and #87. The 18 IDs preserve the original proposal's evidence chains. Locations below use the fourteen-chapter B1 curriculum.

The course's proposed relationship combines three different questions. Can an engineer correct this change using a timely check? Does the integrated product work for its users? Across multiple delivered changes, will enough useful outcomes recur to justify maintaining an engineering capability? The last question involves local cost, responsibility and alternatives. The possible return from use to higher buyer expectations is a hypothesis. None of the sources below establishes the whole relationship.

## R01 • Giffin and colleagues, 2009. Journal of Mechanical Design, industrial case study.

TU Delft author record, abstract, and its account of linked parent, child and sibling change requests by subsystem. [Primary source](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/change-propagation-analysis-in-complex-technical-systems/) · [Related source record](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3149847)

**Supported use.** Changes in one engineering area can trigger work in others. Use the pattern to motivate tracing a specific interface change.

**Scope and placement.** One sensor program across eight years. Its frequency is not an estimate of the demand for updates elsewhere. Ch. 1 for dependency exposure, optional Ch. 4.

## R02 • U.S. Department of Defense, Digital Engineering Strategy, June 2018. Institutional strategy.

Goal 2, printed pp. 8–9, and the surrounding goals on governed model use across the lifecycle. [Primary source](https://www.cto.mil/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Dig-Eng-Strat-2018.pdf)

**Supported use.** Versioned, governed sources have an established engineering purpose. Teams can invest in maintaining these before or alongside agent adoption.

**Scope and placement.** A strategy specifies objectives. It supplies neither a realized saving nor a measure of industrial agent performance. Context for Chs. 1 and 14.

## R05 • Anthropic, December 19, 2024. Provider engineering guidance.

“Agents” and “When (and when not) to use agents.” The agent executes tools and uses their results to choose the next action. [Primary source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents)

**Supported use.** Bounded tool use and environmental feedback are plausible mechanisms for delegation. The chosen complexity should fit the task.

**Scope and placement.** Provider guidance, with examples. It supplies no effect size or independent safety assurance. Chs. 8–9. Cite once where the mechanism matters.

## R06 • Anthropic, September 29, 2025. Provider engineering guidance.

“Context retrieval and agentic search,” particularly lightweight identifiers and just-in-time retrieval. [Primary source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

**Supported use.** Point an agent to the current scenario revision or source record when it needs that input.

**Scope and placement.** Selection and retrieval matter. Context volume alone says little about the adequacy of the information for this decision. Ch. 9 diagnostics, supporting reading for Ch. 14.

## R07 • Anthropic, January 9, 2026. Provider evaluation practice.

Step 2 on unambiguous tasks and reference solutions, and step 5 on grader design and alternative valid behaviors. The discussion explicitly identifies grader faults and unclear tasks. [Primary source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)

**Supported use.** An apparent agent failure can originate in the assignment, evaluation or environment. A maintained check can grow from a recurring observed failure.

**Scope and placement.** Engineering experience with examples. A grader built from the same faulty premise can reward a bad change. Ch. 9 at the diagnostic checkpoint.

## R08 • Ryan Lopopolo, OpenAI, February 11, 2026. Firsthand greenfield product account.

“We started with an empty git repository,” “Increasing application legibility,” and “Enforcing architecture and taste.” The team describes tooling, tests and observability built with agents. [Primary source](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/)

**Supported use.** An agent can help build the environment that it and subsequent agents use. A working application and inspectable traces give the team a basis for specific checks.

**Scope and placement.** One team and product. Its counterfactual time estimate cannot establish results for a multidisciplinary product or for the lab. Ch. 1 figure context, Ch. 14 and the companion where concrete.

## R09 • Lauren Tan, AI Engineer CODE 2026. Firsthand talk, organizer's chapter guide and session notes.

Organizer's timestamped guide and notes on Dune restrictions, runtime inspection, verification maintenance and release-triggered checks. The four chapter starts are 5:00, 9:40, 11:51 and 15:11. ## R10 • METR, November 20, 2026. Independent evaluation report on selected AI research and development tasks.

Section 3.B.2 on task classes where empirical feedback is slow, expensive or hard to generalize. The authors compare agent and expert judgment on selected tasks. [Primary source](https://evals.alignment.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/)

**Supported use.** Task class and quality of feedback matter to a decision to delegate, especially where success cannot be checked cheaply.

**Scope and placement.** Selected research tasks and evaluated model versions. This is a boundary on an inference, not a traffic or industrial-autonomy evaluation. Brief counterweight in Ch. 9 or the companion.

## R11 • Gloaguen and colleagues, arXiv v3, September 29, 2026. Empirical repository-context study.

Abstract, §4.2 and accompanying analysis comparing repository tasks with and without additional context files; version checked against the submission history. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988v3#S4.SS2)

**Supported use.** Providing a repository overview can add cost without reliably resolving more tasks in this sample. Additional context or checks should address a diagnosed need.

**Scope and placement.** The intervention is repository-level context files for coding tasks. It does not test the total value of a connected engineering environment. Ch. 9 or the companion, beside the cost of an intervention.

## R12 • DORA, State of AI-assisted Software Development 2025. Mixed-method industry research.

Executive summary and methods in the full report. DORA reports qualitative fieldwork and survey responses, and relates AI use to organizational conditions. [Primary source](https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/) · [Related source record](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/2025_state_of_ai_assisted_software_development.pdf)

**Supported use.** Teams should examine delivery and review capacity along with generator use. These organizational patterns motivate a local investigation.

**Scope and placement.** Survey and qualitative evidence about software work. Associations do not identify the cause or return of one team's proposed investment. Ch. 1 or companion. Reserve local causal claims for a suitable comparison.

## R13 • DORA, Continuous delivery. Living research-informed practice guidance, version accessed November 23, 2026.

Opening definition and discussion distinguishing continuous delivery from continuous deployment. [Primary source](https://dora.dev/capabilities/continuous-delivery/)

**Supported use.** A release-ready software state enables an authorized deployment later. It informs the comparison with the course's whole-product interval.

**Scope and placement.** Software guidance. The course clock starts at acceptance of an eligible request and ends at the recorded releasability of the identified whole candidate. Ch. 13, next to the measurement definition. Keep the existing DORA metrics link.

## R14 • Demirer, Musolff and Yang. NBER Working Paper 35275, May 2026, revised September 2026. Matched observational event study.

Title page, abstract and description of the event study. The authors relate activity to projects, releases and marketplace use. [NBER paper record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35275) · [Primary source](https://doi.org/10.3386/w35275)

**Supported use.** Estimated changes in activity became smaller farther along the production chain. Choosing a metric closer to delivered use changes the question.

**Scope and placement.** Developer telemetry and marketplace data, not a controlled study of the intersection or a measurement of customer value. The course does not use cross-generation percentages. Ch. 13 or the companion. Link to the stable NBER paper record.

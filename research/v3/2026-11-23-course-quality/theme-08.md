## What the clock captures

Chapter 13 starts the clock when the lead accepts an eligible, bounded request about `IND-UNKNOWN`. The clock stops when the authorized lead records the identified running configuration as releasable with applicable checks, localized manuals, the kit’s catalog entry and the training app. An outstanding service-language review holds the candidate. The lamp’s actual indication still requires a separate investigation. Operations can choose a later installation date. An installation record would establish deployment. A service record could show whether a technician used the procedure and found the right kit. Fewer repeat visits on comparable calls could indicate customer value. The chapter presents an illustrative target. Timing and field outcomes would require observed records. ## What the sources support

In its metrics guide (updated January 5, 2026), DORA’s “Throughput and instability” section defines software change lead time from commit to production deployment for an application or service. Its other measures are deployment frequency, failed deployment recovery time, change fail rate and deployment rework rate. R13, undated practice guidance, distinguishes on-demand readiness from automatic deployment. Its “Common pitfalls” section separates elapsed time, active work and rework. Chapter 13 includes coordination before commit and can stop before a scheduled installation. [DORA metrics](https://dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics/) · [Continuous delivery](https://dora.dev/capabilities/continuous-delivery/)

R12, version 2025.2, combines interviews and a June–July 2025 survey. The executive summary (pp. 3–4) associates AI adoption with higher software throughput and instability. The methods (p. 123) describe the modeled comparisons as observational. Treat the report and both guides as one DORA research program. Independent NASA guidance describes training, manuals and support equipment for product transition (§5.5.1.3, Rev. 2, 2016). Estimating the local return on review routing requires local records. ## Editorial proposal and verification

Keep Chapter 13’s clock and optional DORA link. At the end of Chapter 14’s “Fund the bottleneck that keeps recurring,” ask what would justify earlier language review. Put the comparison in the companion. Match diagnostic changes by scope and required languages before and after early routing. Timestamp acceptance, review request, review start, approval, package check and the authorized decision. Compute elapsed time once from acceptance to decision. Flag open cases. Mark overlapping work and waits on a timeline. Track reviewer person-hours, rework, setup and recurring upkeep separately across repeated changes. Compare manual early notification with automated routing if feasible. An occupied integration rig could absorb the entire gain from earlier approval. Record staffing and rig availability, then check whether the whole-product decision moved.

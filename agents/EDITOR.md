# Editor

## Purpose

Write clear American English for the primary reader named in the chapter's copy contract. Engineer-to-engineer is the default, not a substitute for choosing an audience; technical leaders and executives need the capability, decision and consequence before implementation detail. Lead with concrete decisions. Reduce boilerplate and repeated caveats, retain precise limitations in relevant places. Ensure strong transitions and accessible explanations of specialist methods. Check that every paragraph advances the reader promise and AI appears as an operational rather than late theoretical topic.

## Two-pass chapter edit

Run an **early structure pass** after the copy contract and concept treatments, before full manuscript production. Confirm that the selected direction has a clear reader, promise, message spine, example role, anti-goals and continuity with the course. Reject a direction that depends on the reader already knowing the lab, turns a supporting example into the subject or describes the course when it should address the reader's decision.

Run a **prose-and-alignment pass** after the complete manuscript exists and before illustrations, quiz content and broad preview are treated as final. Read the chapter as a continuous article. Check that the main claim is visible in the opening, section order and conclusion; that paragraphs create forward movement; and that detail, pace and terminology suit the named reader. Preserve technical meaning and evidence boundaries while rewriting for flow.

Use this alignment check in the late pass:

| Element | Editorial acceptance |
|---|---|
| Main message | Can be stated in one sentence and matches the copy contract |
| Example | Makes the argument concrete without becoming the chapter's actual subject |
| Illustration | Explains a central relationship, or is omitted with a reason |
| Quiz | Tests a decision or distinction taught by the final prose |
| Continuity | Opening receives a relevant course thread and the ending hands it forward or closes it |
| Metadata | Title, summary, curriculum map, index and navigation describe the same chapter |

The copy is editorially accepted only when these elements agree. A technically complete manuscript is not automatically ready for visual design, quiz completion or publication.

## Editing adjacent V3 chapters

Use the [current curriculum map](../project/CURRICULUM-MAP.md) and [revised manuscript index](../project/v3/manuscripts/revised/INDEX.md) to locate the active chapters in reading order. Follow the [editorial guide](../project/EDITORIAL-GUIDE.md) for voice and chapter composition.

Before the final preview, read the affected chapters together. Also read the chapter where the selected course through-line was introduced; for a closing chapter this may matter more than only reading its immediate predecessor. Bring the example that makes each decision concrete to the place where the reader first needs it. You may move or shorten passages across the chapters in scope. Read each closing paragraph alongside the next opening. Does the reader carry a question forward, and does the next chapter help answer it? Preserve the technical meaning and keep supporting sources with the claims they explain.

## Shared instructions

Read the [contributor guidance](../AGENTS.md), the relevant chapter briefs and the existing implementation. For V2 work, also consult the V2 tutorial, curriculum, workflow specifications and migration plan. Differentiate historical V0 truth from future aspirations. Implement only an explicitly approved scope. Record exact files, what tests were actually run, uncertainties and decisions. Do not fabricate execution, approvals or deployed features. Defer shared-file merges to reviewer/integrator.

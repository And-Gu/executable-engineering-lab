## Findings for an editorial decision

### LD-01 · Let the measurement question determine whether vehicle identities are needed

**P1 · High confidence.** Chapter 5 says “Mean waiting time would require following individual waits” and its first quiz explains that an average “would require following each vehicle,” and Chapter 6 repeats the condition. The final queue alone cannot answer a mean-wait question. The stronger assertion may lead a team to commission vehicle tracking before choosing its metric. Chapters 7 and 9 explicitly ask about *individual* waits. That more specific question deserves its own design. [Chapter 5, “What does that comparison establish?”][ch5metric] · [Chapter 5 quiz][ch5quiz] · [Chapter 6, “Carry the queues through a second cycle”][ch6metric] · [Chapter 5 source][m05] · [Chapter 6 source][m06] · [Quiz feedback][q05].

**Counterexample.** Define one vehicle-slot for each vehicle waiting just after service in each of the eight slots. I recalculated the NS+EW series from the T1 inputs. Original B gives 2, 2, 4, 4, 3, 2, 2, 2, totaling 21 vehicle-slots. Edited B gives 2, 2, 3, 3, 2, 1, 1, 1, totaling 15. These are reviewer-derived finite-window occupancy totals under an explicit convention. They have no physical duration and leave each unfinished wait open. If an initially empty cohort is followed to clearance with no later arrivals, summing queue occupancy also counts every accrued waiting slot across that cohort without identifying each vehicle. A distribution of individual waits calls for different information. [Inputs][model] · [First-cycle rows][trace] · [Stored edited observation][curation].

**Proposed edit and check.** Replace the Chapter 5 sentence with “One fewer vehicle remains at slot 8. To study delay, specify whose waiting time you want and how to count vehicles still queued. The row-by-row counts can measure accumulated waiting within this window.” Give the distractor the explanation “The difference is a count at one instant. Which vehicles and which intervals would your average cover?” Use the same qualification in Chapter 6. A smaller edit drops the assertion about tracking and keeps the endpoint warning in both chapters and the quiz. The shorter option carries less measurement detail. Preserve the existing fixture, correct quiz key and Chapters 7 and 9. Ask a reader to contrast the two endpoint counts, the finite-window sum and the time until the last vehicle departs. Credit an identified unit, window and outstanding information.

### LD-02 · Make the forward trace answer its own question

**P2 · High confidence.** Chapter 9's radio asks “What changes if we revise the rule?” Its selected path begins “Waiting vehicles still belong to the next slot” and ends with “Existing run record,” which leads to the continuous T1 run. The prose immediately after the figure gives a genuinely changed rule, two independent windows. The backwards trace and caption identify the synthetic result correctly. Inside the forward path, a learner can click through without identifying a changed expectation or the decision that would need fresh evidence. [Public figure and following example][ch9change] · [Pinned figure][threadfig] · [Manuscript][m09] · [Existing run][saved].

**Proposed edit and check.** Use the existing independent-window example in the forward path. Start with the owner's change of objective, keep the first eight reference rows, set the second opening queues to (0,0), predict zero NS departures in B's slot 9, and identify the previous continuous-growth interpretation for review. Label the existing result “Earlier continuous run” and the revised outcome as a prediction until a new run exists. A smaller edit relabels the control “How does the agreed carry rule reach a review?” and leaves the counterfactual to the text below. The first version costs a few revised figure strings, the second sacrifices the in-figure comparison. Keep the present radio controls and backward links. Check both paths using the same B endpoint of two and ask the reader to name the differing row and the run that actually exists.

### LD-03 · Correct the Chapter 13 feedback about technician understanding

**P1 · High confidence.** The keyed feedback for `one-supported-language` says review and link testing “establish that the technician can reach the right instruction and interpret it.” Opening a localized edition identifies the destination. A qualified language reviewer can judge its wording. Neither observation shows what the technician makes of that wording in use. Chapter 11 explicitly gives a case where a specified message passes and a maintainer acts on the wrong interpretation. Chapter 13 itself proposes watching a technician use the procedure. A reader who absorbs the feedback could file two legitimate editorial checks as evidence of task performance. [Chapter 13 quiz][ch13quiz] · [Pinned exact feedback][q13] · [Chapter 13 rehearsal][ch13task] · [Chapter 11 verification and validation][c11].

**Proposed edit and check.** Keep the keyed hold and replace the last sentence with “Open the reviewed edition from this candidate to check the route. The language reviewer judges whether the text preserves the diagnostic meaning. To learn how technicians interpret it, observe one use it during a representative service task.” A smaller option stops after the first two sentences. The fuller version reconnects the existing trial without adding a universal release gate. Keep the assumption that other applicable evidence can carry forward. Ask a reader what to record if the link works, the translation was approved, and a technician nonetheless reports a failed lamp. Credit separate records for routing, editorial judgment and observed task behavior, with a request to investigate the interpretation.

### LD-04 · Keep the contract inspection distinct from a received command in the closing chapter

**P1 · High confidence in the wording, moderate confidence in its effect.** “a software-visible receipt established that a command arrived” reads as a completed event in the intersection. The underlying T1 record compares proposed controller A and adapter B. The executed Chapter 11 excerpt tests a label given an absent observation. It supplies no actual adapter log. Chapter 13 separately states an assumed existing configuration. The closing sentence can reasonably be read as shorthand for the meaning of a receipt, but a reader entering at the synthesis can mistake the promise for an observation. [Chapter 14, “Feedback makes the capability improve”][ch14receipt] · [Manuscript][m14] · [T1 discrepancy and evidence][discrepancy] · [Chapter 11][m11].

**Proposed edit and check.** Use “Comparing the two proposed contracts showed that the adapter promises to acknowledge receipt. Establishing what the lamp displayed would require an independent observation tied to that head and command.” The smaller edit changes “established” to “would establish” and introduces “under the proposed adapter contract.” Keep the example, team decision, two loops and evidence-handoff figure. Ask which artifact exists today, which observation a future receipt would support, and which needs the named head and command. Accept a contract comparison, evidence of receipt under the agreed semantics and a matched independent indication observation, respectively.

### LD-05 · Ask the closing reader to choose an investment when the constraint changes

**P1 · Moderate confidence in the transfer risk, high confidence in the exercise overlap.** The final quiz asks “Which response supports faster whole-product release?” and supplies the same translation delay resolved by Chapter 13's readiness switch and its `next-iteration-investment` item. The funding section itself has several genuinely different constraints and includes maintenance cost. The companion gives readers an excellent comparable-update exercise. A reader can recognize the third key without deciding among two credible investments or saying what would justify changing course. Reading that section may still be sufficient for some experienced readers. [Chapter 13 readiness][c13] · [Chapter 13 quiz source][q13] · [Chapter 14 funding section][ch14fund] · [Final quiz][ch14quiz] · [Pinned item][q14] · [Companion exercise][bonus].

**Proposed edit and check.** Replace just the third item, in the same position before the approved closing paragraphs. For example, “In the last several intersection changes, the rig and manuals were ready on time. Each time, the reviewers spent the start of the rig slot establishing which board and display revisions belonged together. A two-engineer team can try one improvement. What would you try, and what evidence would make it worth maintaining?” Plausible choices are an earlier configuration check, an additional rig slot or earlier language review. Feedback for the first should name a display/board record, its owner, the time spent reconciling subsequent comparable changes and the effort of maintaining the record. Rig capacity becomes a candidate if prepared configurations repeatedly wait for equipment. Language review becomes a candidate if that edition holds release. Ask readers to state a condition that would reverse their first choice before selecting an answer. The scenario is illustrative.

A smaller option leaves all three keys and links the existing “Explore further” directly to “Measure one change of your own” with a specific prompt about the last blocked decision. That adds little text but leaves the self-check unchanged. Retain the numbered ending, both figures, the return to Chapter 1 and the option to keep a rare handoff manual. To verify transfer, change the evidence so prepared configurations are waiting for the *rig*. Ask for a revised investment, its owner and one observation of its benefit or upkeep. The reader's reason matters more than naming a tool. [Existing close][m14] · [Companion source][bonussc].

### LD-06 · Remove an answer-length cue where it masks a decision about evidence

**P2 · High confidence in the count, untested effect on readers.** Across the 38 items, the keyed option is uniquely longest by visible-text character count in **32/38**, and by whitespace-delimited word count in **27/38**. I counted only `options[].text` in the fourteen registered JSON files. The finding describes a shortcut available to a test-taker. It gives no evidence that anyone used it. These are optional formative checks with useful feedback. Equalizing lengths throughout would risk losing precision. The sharpest pilot is Chapter 5's `capture-observation`, where the keyed record is 30 words and both distractors are 15. [Chapter 5 quiz][ch5quiz] · [Pinned item][q05] · [Quiz renderer][quizcomponent].

**Proposed edit and check.** Put the shared provenance in the stem. For example, “All three notes name INT-EXP-001, revision T1, original B, and a post-service queue count in vehicles. Which note preserves the edited run?” Offer comparable records: (a) unchanged inputs and a link labeling one waiting as B's reference result, (b) one NS arrival moved from slot 3 to slot 2, one waiting after slot 8, second cycle proposed, (c) the same move and one waiting recorded at both slots 8 and 16. Feedback for (a) points to the unchanged B result of two. For (c) it identifies the unobserved second horizon. The keyed explanation can point to the saved edit's identity and window. A smaller option shortens only the correct answer after moving common details to the stem. Keep the existing model and item ID. Validate that exactly one record is reproducible with the supplied evidence. Ask a reader to explain why each near miss fails without displaying the score. [Stored observation][curation].

### LD-07 · Fulfill the vocabulary promise on the Learn route

**P2 · High confidence.** Learn says “Chapter 1 introduces systems engineering.” Chapter 1 shows the right multidisciplinary situation and explains the product and engineering systems, but uses no occurrence of the term. Chapter 4 introduces model-based systems engineering in the context of a model. A newcomer following the vocabulary link may mistake the engineering *environment* for the discipline. [Learn, “The course vocabulary”][learn] · [Learn source][learnsrc] · [Chapter 1, “Two systems must evolve together”][ch1two] · [Chapter 4 opening][ch4] · [Curriculum terminology][map].

**Proposed edit and check.** Replace Chapter 1's single transition “Every engineering organization is developing two related systems” with “Systems engineering brings the disciplines, people and operating conditions together when the team makes a product decision. That work develops two related systems.” Keep its existing definitions immediately below. Alternatively, narrow Learn's description to the two systems Chapter 1 already explains. That costs one directory edit but leaves the curriculum's term introduction for later. Ask a reader following the link to identify a systems engineering decision and the capability the team built to make it earlier. The named decision can involve sender, receiver and a physical timing check.

## Surrounding reader paths and verification

Home's two entry links support a reader who wants either the investment argument or the intersection. Its two-system diagram and text name what agents can change and who judges suitability. Learn separates the numbered course, companion, earlier previews and single-sensor lessons. The distinct Lab labels its coverage question and links back to the original experiment. Stage 1 observed the 35-radius reference at 38.52% against a 50% criterion, a zero-radius result, record, restore and clear. Those values apply to the sensor lab alone. Reference opens with the intersection sources and links to the two-cycle experiment before presenting original coverage records and retained CI evidence. Preserve those boundaries. [Home][home] · [Home source][homesc] · [Learn][learn] · [Lab][lab] · [Lab source][labsc] · [Reference][ref] · [Reference source][refsc].

The unnumbered companion gives the small-team reader a concrete counterexample to automation by default. A one-time letter and a monthly bulletin have different economics. One contact record can feed several outputs, while an editor still evaluates meaning. “Measure one change of your own” asks for search effort, hand edits, review effort, rework and recurring maintenance on comparable updates. This provides a bridge to a reader's engineering organization without introducing another recurring V3 case. Its four indicators report different units and populations. Their presence does not demonstrate this course's learning or a team's savings. [Companion exercise][bonus] · [Pinned companion][bonussc].

For a later reader check, give an engineer one changed input and a leader one changed constraint without the answer options. Record what each predicts **before** revealing the figure and which observation prompts a revision. For example, put three EW vehicles into B's slot 8 and keep the usual two new EW arrivals at slot 9. A retained start of (2,3) and an after-service result of (1,5) discriminate EW carry-over. Then switch which resource delays the next complete release and ask for a defensible investment and a maintenance cost. The first has a determinate model answer. The second permits multiple defensible judgments if grounded in the changed evidence. Record task behavior separately from self-report, option recognition and automated verification. These are proposed probes. No human outcomes were collected. [EW case][queuetests] · [Closing discussion][ch14fund].

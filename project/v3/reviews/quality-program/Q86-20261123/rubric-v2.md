## Apply the criteria to a full chapter

1. Follow the incoming engineering decision into the chapter and the concrete question handed forward. Identify which decision belongs to which role, which definition or tool the team maintains, and whether the new capability is needed for the question. Read both neighboring transitions and the relevant sources, figure, exercise and quiz feedback.
2. For a factual claim, state the event, configuration, units, clock or horizon, evidence source and scope of inference where material. Inspect a changed input or missing observation that could distinguish two explanations. State which tests and original controls should be preserved. Label an executed calculation, contract inspection, proposed bench test, fictional release, observed use and reader interpretation accurately.
3. For each material finding give a stable ID, exact excerpt or component and destination, risk, evidence, concrete correction or keep, smaller alternative and cost, dependencies, severity and confidence. P0 is a materially false or path-blocking claim, P1 a consequential impediment to understanding or transfer, and P2 a local improvement. The clarity of the textual evidence does not establish how many readers will be affected.
4. Ask for a decision in a changed case and the next observation that could change it. Give partial credit when only part follows. Place deeper examples where readers need them. Retain a functioning quiz or figure when a new activity adds little. The editor records disposition and issue mapping after the reviewer reports.

The independent reviewer may reach a different conclusion. There is no defect quota or numerical average. Outcomes from the transfer probes require real reader sessions.

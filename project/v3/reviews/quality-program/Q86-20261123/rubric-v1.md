# Editorial review rubric

## Version 1 • Frozen public baseline B1

The reader is a senior engineer, technical leader or executive. The course should help that reader decide what engineering question to investigate, choose credible evidence and judge the investment in the engineering environment. One fictional four-way intersection connects the case. A calculation, a proposed experiment, a CI result, observed product use and a reader's understanding carry different kinds of evidence.

For each chapter trace the incoming question, prerequisite, new engineering decision, evidence, transfer and next question. Follow named inputs, event order, configurations, owners, counterexamples and handoffs. Compare the text with the actual figure, controls, quiz answer and explanatory feedback. Check whether a smaller team could use the distinction. Cite exact B1 source/heading and public route, quote the minimum needed, state what might go wrong for the reader, the evidence, a concrete proposed change or keep, a smaller alternative or cut, costs and dependencies, and an observable check. Rate severity (P0 misleading or core path blocked; P1 impairs material understanding or transfer; P2 local) separately from confidence. A reviewer recommends. The coordinator makes decisions. Retain valuable text when evidence favors it.

The current map and active index govern the corpus. The closing chapter received its own later owner decision. Use the active UK/US terminology from each source and American English in deliverables. Include full scope and inspection limits. A green build, a plausible answer from an agent and a live reader observation must remain distinguishable.

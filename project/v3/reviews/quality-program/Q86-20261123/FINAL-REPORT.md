## What changed for a reader

- Chapter 1 makes a team decide whether a recurring interface error justifies funding and maintaining a check. Catching the error can save rework before release. Only later can a team observe whether a delivered change helped a user. The figure marks a subsequent increase in buyer expectations as a hypothesis. The companion compares the investment with a manual alternative and keeps Lauren Tan’s original embedded talk, including time links for specific examples. [Research and limits for all 18 proposal sources](../../../../../research/v3/2026-11-23-course-quality/reference-register.md)
- Chapter 9 gives five diagnoses before extending an agent’s mandate. An unclear goal, the wrong input revision, a missing test, a defect in the implementation and a changed question call for different responses. An independent verifier caught a regression in the edit. Passing a sensitive check alone could have allowed delegation after a wrong prediction. The corrected text requires both the prediction and the check. [Original finding](verification/check-87.md) · [Independent repair verification](verification/check-87b.md)
- Chapters 5 and 6 let a reader explain why two vehicles remaining after eight slots and 21 vehicle-slots accrued answer different questions. The edited pattern gives one remaining and 15 vehicle-slots. The reader can identify the cohort and observation period needed for a mean, then ask operations for arrivals aligned with the actual signal phases. In the two-cycle exercise, four vehicles enter in eight slots and eight enter in sixteen. Both queues carry over. [Worked arithmetic](verification/check-90.md) · [Final experiment check](verification/check-90b.md)
- Chapters 10–14 now identify who decides, which configuration an observation covers and which product claim it supports. One matched image can show a signal state. Demonstrating a transition also needs evidence of the prior state. Chapter 13 measures from acceptance of an eligible change to the authorized record of whole-product releasability. Repeated, comparable changes can reveal a recurring constraint. Chapter 14 retains its two figures and closing quiz.
